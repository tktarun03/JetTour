# Official spec sheets anchor credible fleet showcase

Manufacturer figures exist for nearly every headline number a fleet showcase app needs (range, cruise speed, ceiling, cabin dimensions, baggage and engines) across twelve popular business-jet types, from the light Embraer Phenom 300E to the ultra-long-range Gulfstream G800. Third-party broker and listing pages, however, regularly print figures that are wrong rather than merely different: mph labelled as knots, statute miles converted as nautical miles, cabin width and height swapped, superseded engines, and spec boxes that contradict their own body text. The app should therefore display manufacturer values wherever they exist and label everything else. Two further cautions shape the data model. First, range figures are quoted under different conditions (from a zero-passenger ferry flight on the Citation CJ4 Gen2 to eight passengers and four crew on the Global 7500), so a range column must carry its conditions. Second, four obvious picks are in transition in 2026: the CJ4 Gen2, Gulfstream G280 and Embraer Praetor 500 have announced successors, and the G650ER has left production. Cabin layouts are the weakest part of the record: manufacturers publish seat counts, zone names and "sleeps N" figures, but no seat-by-seat floorplan was retrieved in text for any aircraft. Prices and charter rates come only from third parties.

## Four popular picks now have successors or have left production

A 2026 app that labels every jet "current" will mislead on four types. The **Gulfstream G650ER is out of production**: the final airframe rolled off the line in **February 2025**, ending a run that began with certification on 7 September 2012 and left almost 600 G650-family jets in service ([AIN](https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/business-aviation/2025-02-12/end-era-last-g650-rolls-production-line)). Gulfstream's G650ER page now redirects to the **G800**, which uses the G650's fuselage ([Gulfstream](https://gulfstream.com/en/aircraft/gulfstream-g650er)). The redirect also means no official G650ER spec sheet could be retrieved, so its commonly quoted figures remain unverified and should not be displayed until they are checked against an archived Gulfstream document.

The **Gulfstream G280 is being replaced by the G300**, announced 30 September 2025 ([FlightGlobal](https://www.flightglobal.com/airframers/gulfstream-reveals-g300-super-midsize-jet-to-replace-g280/164726.article)), and gulfstream.com's G280 URL also now redirects to the G300 page, whose figures are labelled **"preliminary data, subject to change"** ([Gulfstream G300](https://www.gulfstream.com/en/aircraft/gulfstream-g300/)). In the light class, the **CJ4 Gen3**, announced 21 October 2024 with service entry anticipated in 2026, adds Garmin G3000 PRIME avionics, autothrottles and Emergency Autoland to the airframe; Aviation Week quotes the same 2,165 nm range, so airframe and cabin numbers carry over ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/business-aviation/aircraft-overview/aircraft-overview-cessna-citation-cj4)). The **Praetor 500E**, announced 24 February 2026, will not deliver to new customers until **2029**, so the Praetor 500 remains the delivered model ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/business-aviation/aircraft-overview/aircraft-overview-embraers-praetor-500-praetor-600)).

The practical rule is to keep the in-service type when the app showcases a charter fleet, because G650ERs, G280s and CJ4 Gen2s are what operators fly. Each should carry a status tag such as "out of production (2012–2025)" or "succeeded by G300". Choose the successor only if the app represents new-aircraft sales. Bombardier's Global 8000 derivative and Dassault's Falcon 6X were not researched and need their own verification if the app adds them.

## Light and midsize ranges hide very different payload assumptions

The four light and midsize jets share a **45,000 ft ceiling**, but their official ranges are not like-for-like. Embraer quotes the Phenom 300E at **2,010 nm with 5 occupants** ([Embraer via airframer](https://www.airframer.com/news/release/embraer-s-new-enhanced-phenom-300e-receives-anac-easa-and-faa-approval)). Textron's 2,165 nm for the CJ4 Gen2 is a **ferry figure with no passengers**; its own brochure also cites a 2,068 nm IFR range ([Textron CJ4 Gen2 brochure](https://africair.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/CJ4_Gen2_Brochure.pdf)). The Latitude's 2,700 nm assumes **4 passengers at high-speed cruise** ([Textron product card](https://txtav.com/-/media/cessna/files/product-cards/citation/citation_latitude_product_card.pdf)). Showing these side by side without conditions would overstate the CJ4 against the Phenom.

## Super-midsize jets converge on 45,000 ft and a six-foot cabin

The five super-midsize entries are twin Honeywell HTF7000-family jets with **45,000 ft ceilings** and roughly **6 ft stand-up cabins**, so range, width and baggage are what separate them. The **Praetor 600 has the longest range (4,018 nm)**, the **Challenger 3500 the widest cabin (7 ft 2 in)**, the **Longitude the fastest published cruise (483 KTAS)**, and the **G300 the largest stated cabin volume (1,005 cu ft)**. The Longitude's 77 in width is the narrowest of the group, so app copy should not call it the roomiest.

## Broker listings fail through unit swaps and stale data

The third-party errors follow recognisable patterns, which makes them easy to screen for. **Unit mislabelling** is the most common. GlobalAir lists the Praetor 500 and Praetor 600 at "536 kts", which is 466 kt expressed in mph ([GlobalAir Praetor 500](https://www.globalair.com/aircraft-specifications/embraer/praetor-500-specifications/1629); [GlobalAir Praetor 600](https://www.globalair.com/aircraft-specifications/embraer/praetor-600-specifications/1651)). BJT pairs the Praetor 500's 3,340 nm with "5,375 km", a statute-mile conversion; the correct value is about 6,186 km ([BJT](https://bjtonline.com/node/29851)). The Flying Engineer describes Global 7500 space in square feet where cubic feet are meant ([The Flying Engineer](https://theflyingengineer.com/2026/05/27/bombardier-global-7500-explained/)). Air Charter Advisors calls the Falcon 8X's 1,695 cu ft cabin volume its baggage capacity and lists a 600 kt maximum that is inconsistent with M0.90 ([ACA](https://www.aircharteradvisors.com/falcon-8x/)).

**Self-contradiction** is the second pattern. Private Jet Card Comparisons shows 7,005 nm in its Global 7500 spec box against 7,700 nm in its text ([PJCC](https://www.privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/bombardier/global-7500/)), 3,277 nm against 3,400 nm for the Challenger 3500 ([PJCC](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/bombardier/challenger-3500/)), and two different G700 cabin heights ([PJCC G700](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/gulfstream/g700/)). GlobalAir lists the G280 width as both 7 ft 2 in and 6 ft 11 in, and swaps the Praetor 500's width and height. **Stale data** is the third: Magellan lists the Phenom 300E with the superseded 3,360 lb PW535E engine rather than the 3,478 lb PW535E1, and PlanePHD's 1,971 nm range and 453 kt cruise appear to describe the original Phenom 300 ([PlanePHD](https://planephd.com/research/embraer-phenom-300e)).

The passenger field needs the most care, because third parties mix three different quantities: certified maximum occupancy, marketed maximum passengers, and seats in a particular charter aircraft. Magellan's "6 passengers" for the Phenom 300E is an operator figure, not the certified 9 or 10 ([Magellan Jets](https://magellanjets.com/aircraft/embraer-phenom-300e)). In the large class, no manufacturer basis exists for round figures such as "13 typical / 14 max": the makers publish "up to 19" or "12–16", and only the G700's Grand Suite layout seats 13 ([Gulfstream G700](https://www.gulfstream.com/en/aircraft/gulfstream-g700)). The app should store certified or marketed maximum and "as configured" seats as separate fields and never present a tail-specific count as a type specification.

## Seat maps are sourced only at the zone level

No manufacturer seat-by-seat floorplan was retrieved in text for any of the twelve types. Brochures contain floorplan images, and Gulfstream's are on its site, but none were machine-readable. What is sourced is the **inventory of elements**, and that supports credible "typical layout" diagrams if they are labelled as illustrations.

The light jets share a single-zone plan. Textron confirms the CJ4 Gen2 has a forward refreshment centre, a side-facing seat or two-place couch opposite the door, four executive tables and a **belted, externally serviced aft lavatory** ([Textron brochure](https://africair.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/CJ4_Gen2_Brochure.pdf)). The Phenom 300E combines a four-chair club, extra forward-facing seats and a two-place side-facing divan; its 10-passenger maximum depends on that divan, the belted lavatory seat and the co-pilot seat ([PlanePHD](https://planephd.com/research/embraer-phenom-300e); [Aviation Week](https://ngstage.aviationweek.com/business-aviation/aircraft-overview/aircraft-overview-phenom-300e)). The only Latitude layout found is one 2016 aircraft for sale: a four-seat centre club, a forward right-hand two-place couch and a belted lavatory seat, listed as 8 seats although only 7 are named ([Jet Access](https://www.flyjetaccess.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Citation-Latitude_February-2026-Aircraft-Brokerage-Spec-Sheet-1.pdf)). **No Praetor 500 layout was found at all**, so the app should not draw one without an Embraer floorplan.

Super-midsize cabins follow a **two-zone "double club"** pattern: two facing pairs forward and two aft for 8 seats, with a three-place side-facing divan replacing two aft club seats to reach 9 ([PJCC](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/bombardier/challenger-3500/)). Gulfstream publishes the G300's official configurations as **10 passengers sleeping 5, 9 sleeping 4, and 8 sleeping 4** across two living areas with an aft vacuum lavatory ([Gulfstream G300](https://www.gulfstream.com/en/aircraft/gulfstream-g300/)). The Praetor 600 offers two-zone seating for 8 or 9, an optional three-place berthing divan, facing seats that combine into beds to **sleep 4**, and an aft lavatory with an electric solid door ([BJT](https://bjtonline.com/business-jet-news/a-thumbs-up-for-embraers-praetor-600)). Textron calls the Longitude's seating "fully berthable" with an optional aft couch and side-facing seat but gives no bed count ([Textron](https://cessna.txtav.com/en/citation/longitude)). The G300's "10 windows" reading is suspect and should be checked before display.

The **Global 7500 is the best-documented showcase cabin**. Bombardier's brochure names, behind a forward galley, lavatory and crew suite, a Club Suite of 4 Nuage seats, a Conference Suite with a 6-seat dining table, an Entertainment Suite with a 3-place berthable divan, and a Private Suite with a divan and Nuage seat (or a full-size bed in the Principal Suite option), followed by an aft en suite with an available stand-up shower ([Bombardier](https://bombardier.com/sites/default/files/2024-04/Global7500-Brochure.pdf)). Those zones add up to **17 passenger seats**. The front-to-back order is inferred, since the brochure text does not state it. A conflicting third-party description with a "king-size" bed and a rear crew suite contradicts Bombardier and should be discarded ([The Flying Engineer](https://theflyingengineer.com/2026/05/27/bombardier-global-7500-explained/)). Gulfstream lists the G700 as Forward Galley plus Grand Suite (13 passengers, sleeps 8), Ultragalley plus crew compartment (19, sleeps 9) or five living areas (18, sleeps 9), and the G800 as 15 passengers with a crew compartment or 17 without, each sleeping 7 ([G700](https://www.gulfstream.com/en/aircraft/gulfstream-g700); [G800](https://www.gulfstream.com/en/aircraft/gulfstream-g800/)). Dassault describes a typical Falcon 8X **three-lounge cabin with an aft shower**, a choice of entryway with crew rest or "grand galley", and an optional Privacy Suite ([Dassault](https://www.dassaultfalcon.com/aircraft/falcon-8x/)), but per-lounge seat types were not found.

## Prices and charter rates rest entirely on third-party estimates

No manufacturer publishes a list price; Textron's Latitude page simply says to contact sales ([Textron](https://cessna.txtav.com/citation/latitude)). The figures that exist are aggregator or press estimates: **Praetor 500 $18.395M** (2024 list, per GlobalAir), **Praetor 600 $21.995M** and **G280 $24.5M** (Q2 2025, GlobalAir), **Challenger 3500 about $26.7–27M** ([PJCC](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/bombardier/challenger-3500/)), **Falcon 8X $59.3M MSRP** ([ACA](https://www.aircharteradvisors.com/falcon-8x/)), **G700 $78M** ([PJCC](https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/gulfstream/g700/)) and **Global 7500 $75–81M** before $7–15M of completion ([PJCC](https://www.privatejetcardcomparisons.com/aircraft/bombardier/global-7500/); [The Flying Engineer](https://theflyingengineer.com/2026/05/27/bombardier-global-7500-explained/)). No sourced price was found for the Phenom 300E, CJ4 Gen2, Latitude, Longitude, G300 or G800.

Charter rates are only sourced by class, and they vary widely. Blade's 2026 guide gives **light $2,500–4,500/hr, midsize $4,000–7,000/hr and super-midsize $6,000–9,000/hr**, billed wheels-up to wheels-down, with total trip cost typically **20–40% above** the base rate once fees and taxes are added ([Blade](https://www.blade.com/cheapest-private-jet-flights)). The super-midsize jet-card average reached **$12,485/hr in Q3 2026** including fuel surcharges and 7.5% FET, which shows how far all-in costs sit above base charter bands ([The Flying Engineer](https://theflyingengineer.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-rent-a-private-jet/)). For ultra-long-range jets, rates climb **beyond $14,000/hr** ([Destination.com](https://www.destination.com/news/private-jet-hourly-rates-range-2500-to-14000-in-2026)). Air Charter Advisors puts the Falcon 8X at $14,000–17,000 ([ACA](https://www.aircharteradvisors.com/falcon-8x/)), while Blade gives the G700 $11,500–13,500 and contradicts itself on the G650, quoting both $10,000–12,500 and $13,000–17,000 on one page ([Blade](https://www.blade.com/gulfstream-charter-cost)). A defensible display is a class band with an "estimate, excludes taxes and fees" label, not a per-aircraft figure.

## Conclusion

The verification burden in this dataset sits mainly in the field definitions, not in the headline numbers. Range, passengers and cabin length each describe different quantities depending on who publishes them: range with zero, four or eight passengers; occupants versus passengers versus seats in one tail; cabin length to the cockpit divider versus excluding baggage. An app that stores a value without its condition and source tag will drift back into exactly the errors broker sites make. A schema with `value`, `conditions`, `basis` (official / manufacturer-via-press / third-party / calculated) and `as_of` fields addresses most of the problem.

The remaining work is narrow and identifiable. Before the showcase goes live, it needs Embraer's own Phenom 300E cabin cross-section, an archived G650ER spec sheet, the Global 7500's cabin and baggage volumes, a check of the G300 window count, and manufacturer floorplan images for every type, because no seat-by-seat layout in this record is better than a labelled illustration. The large-cabin flagships, ironically the most expensive aircraft, are the safest to publish today. Their makers quote comparable ranges and complete cabin figures, while the cheaper light and midsize jets carry the most third-party gaps.
